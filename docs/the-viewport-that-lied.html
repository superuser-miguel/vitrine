The viewport that lied

A months-old feeling — opening a big folder is sluggish, like it starts in the middle — in Vitrine, a GTK4/Rust image browser. It took three rebuilds and one confidently wrong fix to find that the app was lying to itself about where you were looking. The line that ended it printed a single number.

July 2026 · GNOME · Rust · GTK4 · virtualized lists

Vitrine shows images in a GtkGridView — a virtualized grid, so a folder of 7,493 photos only ever builds a few dozen cell widgets, recycling them as you scroll. Thumbnails decode lazily on a bounded worker, and the decoder is picky about order: it loads the cell nearest the middle of what you're looking at first, then fans outward. Fill the screen you're staring at before the stuff you'd have to scroll to. Sensible.

The complaint was old and vague, the way the real ones are: opening a large folder feels slow, and it's like it decodes from the middle instead of the top. Every previous look had ended the same way — "the decoder starts from the centre, that's by design" — and the feeling never went away. This time we measured it, and the tidy explanation fell apart twice before it stood up.

1The confident wrong answer

A fix shipped before a measurement is a guess with a commit hash.

The decoder logs which cell it fills and where it thinks the viewport is centred. On a cold open of the 7,493-image folder, the very first lines were:

VDBG-GRIDFILL ms=0   pos=111 center=112 visible=true
VDBG-GRIDFILL ms=101 pos=112 center=112 visible=true
VDBG-GRIDFILL ms=109 pos=113 center=112 visible=true

Position 111, 112, 113 — with the grid five icons wide, that's row 22. The decoder opened a fresh folder and immediately started work seventeen rows below the top. The obvious reading: the grid scrolls to the middle of the directory on open, and the decoder is faithfully serving whatever garbage viewport it was handed.

So I fixed the scroll. After the folder populates, tell the grid to go home:

// populate(): land on the first item of the sort, not row 22
grid.scroll_to(0, ListScrollFlags::empty(), None);

Rebuilt the Flatpak, ran a hands-off cold open, measured. Still center=112. Added a deferred re-issue in case the grid wasn't mapped yet. Rebuilt. Still 112. A third rebuild with a probe on the scroll fix to prove it even ran. It ran. Still 112.

Where I went wrong

I wrote an elegant fix for a bug I hadn't confirmed existed. scroll_to(0) assumes the grid is somewhere other than the top — and I never checked whether it was. Three rebuilds went into correcting a scroll position that, it turns out, was already correct.

2One probe, one number

Instrument the assumption, not the symptom.

The scroll fix logged the scrollbar's value each time it fired, mostly as an afterthought. That afterthought was the whole investigation:

VDBG-GRIDHOME scroll_to(0) n=7543 vadj=0

vadj=0. The vertical scroll adjustment was at zero — the top — the entire time. The grid never opened in the middle. It was at the top, exactly where it should be, while the decoder cheerfully insisted the centre of attention was position 112.

Two facts that couldn't both be true, and the scroll fix had been quietly assuming the false one. I reverted it. The bug was never where the grid was pointed; it was what the decoder believed about where I was looking — and those had come apart.

Lesson

Three rebuilds of theorizing ended the moment one eprintln! printed the number the theory depended on. When a fix doesn't move the needle, don't build a bigger fix — print the assumption underneath it.

3The centre that was never there

Don't ask the widgets what's visible. Ask the scrollbar.

So where did 112 come from? The decoder needs to know the centre of the viewport to prioritize around it, and here is how it worked it out — from the cells GtkGridView had handed it via the factory's bind callback:

// the viewport centre, inferred from the cells GTK bound
let lo = pending.iter().map(|c| c.position).min().unwrap();
let hi = pending.iter().map(|c| c.position).max().unwrap();
visible_center = (lo + hi) / 2;   // ← the lie

It reads like common sense: the visible cells are the ones GTK bound, so their midpoint is the middle of the screen. But a virtualized list binds cells on its schedule, not yours. At default 240 px thumbnails in a 1100×720 window only about ten cells are genuinely on screen — yet over the open, the bind callback fired for cells all the way across positions 0 to 224. Midpoint: 112. The heuristic didn't measure the viewport; it measured GTK's binding behaviour and mistook it for the viewport.

The consequence is quietly perverse. With the "centre" pinned at 112, the ten cells you can actually see — positions 0 to 11, top-left, exactly where your eyes land — are the furthest from the centre the decoder is optimizing for. They are decoded almost last. The scrollbar is at the top, your eyes are at the top, and the decoder is grinding through off-screen cells first. That is the "sluggish, feels like the middle" — not a scroll position, but a decode order chasing a phantom centre.

The decoder's ordering was never wrong. It was fed a poisoned number. The fix is to stop inferring the viewport from bound widgets and read it from the thing that actually knows — the scroll adjustment:

// where the eye actually is: derived from the scrollbar, not the cells
fn viewport_center_position(&self) -> u32 {
    let adj = self.grid_scroller.vadjustment();
    let frac = (adj.value() + adj.page_size() / 2.0) / adj.upper();
    (frac * n_items as f64).round() as u32
}

At vadj=0 that resolves to the top, where it belongs. To keep the change honest it was verified the same hands-off way the bug was found — an A/B of two cold opens, one on a built-in sort and one on a Lua script sort, to confirm the fix wasn't sort-dependent:

BeforeAfter
Opening decode centrepos 112pos 2
First 12 cells decodedpos 104–121pos 0–13
Sort-dependent?no (112 either way)no
Lesson

Bound list items are an implementation detail of the virtualization, not a description of the viewport. If you need to know what the user can see in a GtkListView or GtkGridView, ask the scroll adjustment — value, page size, upper — not the set of cells that happen to be alive.

4The part I couldn't prove — then did

A fifty-line reproducer, and a number that isn't an accident.

The fix worked because it stopped trusting the bound cells. It did not explain why the bind callback fired across 225 positions for a ten-cell viewport. That 225 was an interpretation of my own log, and I wasn't going to claim "GtkGridView over-binds twenty times its viewport" from a Vitrine trace — that's the kind of thing a maintainer corrects you on, and rightly. So I built the smallest thing that could settle it: a ~60-line PyGObject grid — a 10,000-item StringList, plain label cells, a counter on the factory's bind and unbind — no Rust, no Vitrine, no image decoding. Just: how many cells does it bind at rest?

It binds a fixed 32 rows. Not proportional to the viewport — fixed. The count is invariant to window height, cell size and model size, and scales only with the column count, exactly as 32 × columns + 1:

columns1234567
cells bound336597129161193225

Seven columns → 225. That is Vitrine's number to the digit, because Vitrine's grid caps at MAX_COLUMNS = 7 — and the midpoint of that 0–224 band is 112, the exact poisoned centre from §3. The mystery number was never mysterious: it was 32 × 7 + 1. And total_bind_events == live_bound in the probe, so these are 225 simultaneous live widgets, not recycling churn. For a grid showing three rows, GtkGridView realizes thirty-two — roughly a 10× buffer, held.

Two honest edges remain. This is host GTK 4.22 (Vitrine's Flatpak runtime differs — but the exact 225 match says the same behaviour is in it). And I couldn't fit a viewport taller than 32 rows on this display, so whether 32 is a floor or a hard cap above 32 visible rows is untested; for any ordinary thumbnail grid it's a fixed 32-row floor. The reproducer is in the repo (docs/repro/) if you want to run it against your own GTK.

Lesson

Flag the cause you didn't prove — then go prove it. The gap between "I observed 225 in my app" and "GtkGridView binds 32 × columns + 1 in sixty lines of anyone's Python" is the whole difference between a hunch and a finding, and it was one afternoon's work to cross. Don't publish the hunch when the finding is that close.

The feeling had been real for months and the cause had been misfiled just as long — a perception blamed on the decoder, when the decoder was only ever obeying a number computed from the wrong thing. The whole fix is a function that asks the scrollbar instead of the cells. The whole lesson is to print the number your theory rests on before you write the fix that rests on your theory.
