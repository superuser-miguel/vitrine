## The decode investigation — how it resolved (lesson worth keeping)

The months-old "it opens in the middle / sluggish" complaint. The diagnosis
changed **twice**:

1. **Wrong turn.** Hypothesised the grid opens mid-directory; wrote a
   `scroll_to(0)`-on-populate fix; **reverted it** when a `VDBG-GRIDHOME` probe
   showed `vadj=0` — the grid was always at the top.
   *Lesson: instrument before "fixing." Three flatpak rebuilds were burned on the
   phantom; the probe is what caught it.*
2. **Real cause — V-25.** On initial realize `GtkGridView` binds ~225 cells for a
   ~10-cell viewport; `flush_pending` set `visible_center = (lo+hi)/2` of the
   *bound* range = 112, so the actually-visible **top** cells decoded near-last —
   the top sat blank while the decoder ground through off-screen cells. **Fixed**
   by deriving the centre from the vadjustment (`viewport_center_position()`),
   with a bound-range fallback before geometry is ready. Verified with
   `ab-openpos.sh` (A/B, hands-off, cold): opening centre **112 → 2**, first
   fills all pos 0–13, sort-independent. The decoder's ordering was never wrong —
   it was fed a poisoned centre.
3. **V-26 (filmstrip).** Grid + filmstrip loads share `decode_gate()` (4–8
   permits); with the viewer up, the invisible grid still kept ~24 decodes in
   flight vs the filmstrip's 8. **Fixed:** `pump_loads` stands down while the
   viewer page is visible (queue kept, in-flight drains); a nav-view
   `visible_page` notify re-pumps on return to the browser.

**Leftover (unscheduled curiosity, not a bug):** why GtkGridView over-binds ~225
cells for a ~10-cell viewport at all — wasted bind work regardless of decode
order. Noted, not chased.
